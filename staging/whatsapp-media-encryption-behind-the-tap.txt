Priya taps send on a photo of her cat.
Before her friend's phone buzzes, whatsapp media encryption locks the image with a key only those two phones will ever hold.
The servers in between see nothing except scrambled noise. 📸

WhatsApp handles media by completely separating the encrypted asset upload from the transmission of the decryption key.

Here is where it bites:
→ Storing unencrypted media on application servers exposes user files during a breach
→ Encrypting large media directly through end-to-end ratchets exhausts device memory
→ Passing keys through media proxies allows the hosting platform to inspect content

1. Your phone compresses the photo locally and generates a fresh 32-byte symmetric AES-CBC key.
2. The image bytes are encrypted and uploaded to an ordinary blob storage server as pure ciphertext.
3. Your device sends the download link and AES key inside a standard end-to-end encrypted Signal message.
4. The recipient's phone fetches the raw blob, validates the SHA-256 hash, and decrypts the image locally.

Separating the media payload from the ratchet message keeps storage costs low while ensuring zero cloud visibility across one hundred billion daily messages.
