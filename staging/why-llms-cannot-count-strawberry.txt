Your model has never seen the letter R.
It only sees numbers.
And that blind spot costs real money. 🍓

Tokens are the discrete integer chunks that text gets compressed into before a neural network ever sees it.

Here is where it bites:
→ Counting characters fails because syllables exist as single atomic IDs.
→ Non-English API bills run up to three times higher for the same text.
→ Context windows shrink drastically when processing non-Latin scripts.

When text enters a pipeline, a tokenizer runs Byte Pair Encoding. It starts with single bytes and iteratively merges the most frequent pairs found in the training data into a vocabulary of roughly one hundred thousand tokens.

Because the word strawberry appears often, the tokenizer compresses it into just two tokens: straw and berry. The transformer's attention heads compute math across those two tokens, completely unaware of the individual letters inside. If a model gets the count right, it is simply matching training patterns, not inspecting letters.

For languages like Hindi, Japanese, or Arabic, rare byte combinations do not get merged. A single letter can cost three separate byte tokens, running your latency and context costs through the roof.
