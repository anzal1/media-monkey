Chunking is the process of breaking long documents into bounded pieces before calculating vector embeddings for retrieval.

Picture this:
↳ A fixed 500-token window splits a financial balance sheet midway through Q3 revenue.
↳ The pronoun 'it' lives in chunk B while its actual subject sits stranded in chunk A.
↳ The embedding model calculates vectors for fragments that carry half-truths.

When text gets chopped blindly by character counts, semantic coherence evaporates. An embedding model maps full thoughts into vector space; feed it half a sentence, and its mathematical coordinates float away from the actual query.

Production architectures drop static character splitters. They use semantic chunking to track running cosine similarity between adjacent sentences, breaking only when topics shift. Or they use parent-child indexing: embed small child chunks for precise retrieval, but return the whole parent section to your LLM context window.

Stop blaming your embedding model for bad chunking boundaries. Smarter splits substantially boost your retrieval recall without touching model weights.
