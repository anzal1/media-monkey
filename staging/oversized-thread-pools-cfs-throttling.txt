Your CPU usage sits at twenty percent.
Your P99 latency is suddenly three seconds.
Your pods are healthy on paper.

CFS quota throttling is the Linux kernel stopping your container mid-flight because you used your time slice too fast.

Here is where it bites:
→ Java or Go spins up dozens of threads by host core count
→ A burst lands and wakes forty worker threads in parallel
→ Your CPU quota runs dry in three milliseconds flat

Kubernetes controls CPU limits using the Completely Fair Scheduler bandwidth controller. It chops execution into hundred-millisecond slices. If you give a container one core of limit, it gets exactly one hundred milliseconds of CPU time per slice.

Run forty threads at the exact same moment on a big forty-eight core node, and they consume forty milliseconds of CPU time for every single millisecond of real-world time. Your entire hundred-millisecond budget disappears in two and a half milliseconds. The kernel throttles the whole cgroup, freezing every thread for the next ninety-seven milliseconds.

Your monitoring looks innocent because average utilization over sixty seconds is tiny. But requests freeze, database connections time out, and tail latency explodes.

Match your worker pools to your actual CPU limits or drop CPU limits entirely and rely on CPU requests.

Keep this handy next time your tail latency goes vertical.
