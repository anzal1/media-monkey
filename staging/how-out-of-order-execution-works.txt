Out of order execution broke your lock-free queue 💥

Out of order execution is the hardware mechanism where a CPU runs instructions as their operands become available rather than following your sequential source code.

Here is where it bites:
→ A background thread reads a pointer before its data fields initialize
→ Your lock-free ring buffer drops writes under heavy load
→ High-contention algorithms desync despite correct sequential code

The instruction fetcher reads your binary sequentially, and the decoder breaks assembly into micro-operations. Register renaming maps your static variables onto hundreds of physical registers to eliminate false data dependencies. Work sits inside the reservation station until operands exist; while one operation waits hundreds of cycles on DRAM, ready independent instructions fire immediately.

Everything finishes in a reorder buffer. The hardware guarantees in-order retirement to single-threaded observers, preserving precise interrupts. But across multiple cores, those reordered loads and stores become visible unless you insert memory barriers.

A single cache miss costs about 200 clock cycles. Without out of order execution, modern silicon would idle over ninety percent of the day waiting on DRAM.

Remember this next time you write concurrency primitives without atomic fences.
