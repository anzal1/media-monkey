Cyclomatic complexity measures the number of linearly independent paths through your code's control flow graph.

Here is where it bites:
→ A simple helper function starts with two distinct execution branches.
→ You add an includeArchived flag to satisfy a single new caller.
→ Next quarter someone adds a sendNotification boolean right beside it.

Suddenly your innocent helper holds four completely divergent execution paths. At every call site, reading processUser(id, true, false) gives no clue about what those flags actually trigger without jumping straight to the definition.

McCabe's metric proves that your testing obligations multiply with every binary branch you introduce. Three flags turn one simple routine into eight separate paths that all require independent test fixtures to prevent regressions.

Splitting the shared logic into two explicit functions keeps cyclomatic complexity low and eliminates mystery boolean arguments across your codebase.
