Disk IOPS pin at one hundred percent after an innocent patch.
Postgres autovacuum spins out of control trying to keep up with dead tuples.
You only updated a tiny unindexed status flag.

Postgres uses multi-version concurrency control, meaning an update is always a hidden insert of a brand-new row version at a new physical storage location.

Here is where it bites:
→ The target column has no index of its own
→ The table page is packed tight with no free space
→ Every single secondary index must log a new leaf pointer

When a row version moves to a separate disk page, Heap-Only Tuple optimization fails. Secondary indexes do not point to primary keys; they point straight to raw physical disk addresses. When the physical location changes, every index tree on that table must record the new item pointer.

A single update on a table with eight indexes turns into nine separate disk writes, leaving piles of dead tuples behind for autovacuum to clean up.

Lowering your table fillfactor from one hundred to eighty-five or ninety leaves enough free space inside each page for HOT updates to chain locally without touching a single index.
