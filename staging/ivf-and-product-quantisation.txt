One billion raw embeddings require 4TB of memory.
Uncompressed floats crush your vector search budget.
Running product quantisation shrinks that footprint to 100GB.

Product quantisation is a lossy compression algorithm that cuts high-dimensional vectors into segments and maps each segment to a shared codebook centroid.

Picture this:
→ Storing uncompressed vectors causes out-of-memory crashes
→ HNSW graph indexes require massive amounts of dedicated RAM
→ Your similarity queries slow to an unworkable crawl

First, inverted file partitioning groups the dataset into Voronoi cells using k-means. A query vector checks only nearby cluster centroids, pruning ninety-nine percent of candidates immediately.

Then, product quantisation slices each vector into sub-vectors. Each sub-vector maps to its closest codebook centroid, turning floating-point arrays into a compact sequence of single-byte indices.

During inference, the system builds an asymmetric distance lookup table. Distances are calculated against the codebook instead of rebuilding raw floats, slashing memory footprint by up to ninety-seven percent.
