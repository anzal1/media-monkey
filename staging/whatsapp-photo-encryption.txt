WhatsApp media encryption separates the heavy image storage from the cryptographic keys that unlock it.

Picture this:
→ A cloud server hosting petabytes of pictures it cannot read
→ A subpoena requesting user uploads returning useless static
→ Two phones exchanging tiny secret keys without leaking them

1. Your phone generates a fresh 32-byte symmetric key right on your device.
2. The image is encrypted locally using that key into an unreadable binary blob.
3. Your device uploads the scrambled blob to a media delivery server.
4. The server stores the payload and returns an ordinary download link.
5. Your phone wraps that link and the secret key inside an end-to-end encrypted message via the Signal Protocol.
6. The recipient phone fetches the encrypted blob and unpacks the pixels locally.

Central servers handle hundreds of millions of media transfers every single day without holding the cryptographic authority to inspect what passes through their hard drives.
