Silent udp packet drops are deleting traffic before your app ever sees it.

The UDP receive buffer is a fixed kernel queue that dumps packets without warning when userspace reads too slowly.

Here is where it bites:
→ Traffic doubles during a flash sale
→ Runtimes pause for a short memory sweep
→ Clients hang waiting for responses that never arrive

Incoming datagrams travel from the network card directly into socket memory. The Linux kernel assigns this queue a default byte limit defined by rmem_default. Unlike TCP, UDP carries no congestion windows or backoff signals. When that buffer hits its ceiling, incoming packets drop on the floor instantly.

Your metrics flatline because your application continues reading at its maximum processing speed. You measure steady performance while discarding half your customer traffic behind the scenes.

Fix it by raising net.core.rmem_max, sizing up SO_RCVBUF, and draining packets in batches using the recvmmsg system call.
