Priya taps send on a photo of her cat.
Before her friend's phone rings, the file vanishes from her screen.
WhatsApp media encryption ensures the server never actually sees the image. 📸

WhatsApp separates the delivery of heavy media files from the delivery of the message itself, keeping both ends zero-knowledge.

Here is where it bites:
➔ Uploading raw photos over chat sockets kills network throughput
➔ Encrypting massive files directly through Double Ratchet sessions is computationally expensive
➔ Central servers storing unencrypted user media create massive security liabilities

1. The phone compresses the photo locally and creates a random 32-byte symmetric AES-GCM key.
2. The raw image is encrypted into opaque ciphertext on the device.
3. The phone uploads the scrambled file to a media server and gets back a download link.
4. The decryption key, SHA-256 hash, and URL get packaged into a metadata message.
5. That metadata travels across the Signal Protocol channel to the recipient.
6. The recipient's phone fetches the encrypted blob from the link and decrypts the pixels locally.

Your photos never exist as readable images on WhatsApp's hardware, meaning even a complete database dump yields nothing but scrambled bytes.

Next time you build an upload pipeline, remember this split.
