Your write was confirmed by three out of five nodes.
A rolling restart triggers a new leader election.
The raft consensus algorithm discards your data anyway. ⚠️

Raft guarantees safety by preventing a new leader from ever overwriting a committed log entry.

Here is where it bites:
→ A leader writes an entry during term two and immediately crashes
→ A term four leader resumes copying that older entry to a quorum
→ A term three follower still collects enough votes to overwrite it

Figure 8 of the original Raft paper outlines this exact vulnerability. Even when an older entry reaches a majority of servers, another candidate with higher term entries can still gather a voting majority from nodes that missed it. If the term four leader considered that replicated term two entry committed, the incoming term five leader would erase history that clients were told was safe.

Raft eliminates this paradox by forbidding a leader from committing older term entries directly by counting replicas. The leader must replicate an entry from its current term to a quorum. The moment that current term entry achieves majority replication, every earlier log entry behind it commits indirectly by induction.

Violating this invariant leads to silent log divergences and state machine inconsistencies under heavy partition stress.
