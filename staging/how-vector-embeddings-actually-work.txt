Your model uses vector embeddings to map raw strings into high-dimensional geometric coordinates.
Text goes in.
Numbers come out.
Similar concepts sit together.

Vector embeddings are dense arrays of floating point numbers that represent the underlying semantic meaning of text inside a continuous geometric coordinate space.

Here is where it bites:
→ Storing raw strings lets you match keywords, but misses synonyms entirely.
→ Brute force vector comparisons melt your CPU at scale.
→ Flat token IDs contain no usable geometric relationships for similarity.

Text first gets chopped into token integers. Those integers look up fixed rows inside a massive weight matrix called an embedding table. At this stage, words have isolated coordinates, but no context.

The model passes these coordinates through transformer attention layers, shifting their position in space based on neighboring words. The static representation of a word rotates into its contextual meaning. Once vectors exist, comparing two ideas is just calculating the cosine angle between two directional arrows.

Searching ten million vectors brute force takes hundreds of milliseconds. Building an HNSW graph index drops that query time to under five milliseconds by navigating spatial neighborhoods hierarchically.
