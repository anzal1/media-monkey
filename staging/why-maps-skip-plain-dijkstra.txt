Fifty million intersections across a continent.
Your server searches shortest paths in every direction.
Latency spikes to four seconds while the app freezes 📍

Contraction Hierarchies solve this by precomputing shortcut edges across road networks so queries only touch major transit arteries.

Here is where it bites:
→ Running raw Dijkstra searches millions of residential dead ends
→ Memory exhausts while priority queues hold every side street
→ Real-time navigation stalls when traffic recalculates on the road

Textbook pathfinding evaluates edges uniformly like ink spreading on paper. It inspects thousands of neighborhood streets heading north even when you are driving south.

Production engines like OSRM contract the graph beforehand. They remove minor residential vertices and insert direct shortcut edges across highway intersections. At runtime, a bidirectional search climbs into national highway networks from both ends and meets in the middle.

You cut query exploration from fifty million nodes down to under one thousand, dropping latency from four seconds to under two milliseconds.

Remember this before running ad hoc pathfinding on raw geographic graphs.
