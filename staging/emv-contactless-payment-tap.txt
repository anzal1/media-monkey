Sofia taps her card for a morning espresso.
Before she pulls her hand back, the terminal beeps to confirm the local radio exchange is finished.
Under five hundred milliseconds of EMV contactless payment cryptography just ran off thin air.

An EMV contactless card is an unpowered microcontroller that uses magnetic induction to compute dynamic symmetric proof for a transaction.

Picture this:
→ A card sitting in your wallet has zero battery and runs no background processes.
→ A skimming coil nearby tries to power the chip up without physical contact.
→ The terminal must verify the card is authentic before the buyer walks out.

1. The reader broadcasts an electromagnetic field at 13.56 MHz, inducing a tiny electric current in the card antenna that boots the secure chip.
2. The terminal transmits a SELECT command matching an Application Identifier to pick Visa, Mastercard, or Amex.
3. The reader sends an unpredictable number, the store amount, and the currency code into the card.
4. The chip bumps its internal Application Transaction Counter and runs a symmetric cipher with a shared issuer secret key to produce an Application Request Cryptogram.
5. The card hands over the cryptogram and account details, and the reader beeps so you can remove your plastic.
6. The terminal sends an ISO 8583 network payload across the acquiring switch to the issuing bank to validate the counter and approve the funds.

Replaying a captured contactless payload fails because the bank drops any reused transaction counter immediately.
