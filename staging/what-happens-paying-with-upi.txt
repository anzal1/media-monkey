Aisha scans a paper QR code at a tea stall.
She enters forty rupees and taps confirm.
Paying with UPI takes two seconds, but the app on her screen never touched the money.

UPI is a national financial switch orchestrated by NPCI that coordinates real-time messaging between two independent banks without either bank exposing credentials.

Here is where it bites:
→ A customer assumes the payment app stores their money
→ The merchant uses a completely different bank than the buyer
→ A payment must fail safely if the second bank times out

1. The app reads the QR code and asks NPCI's switch to resolve the merchant's virtual payment address into a destination account.
2. The user enters their UPI PIN inside an isolated common library, encrypting it with NPCI's key before NPCI translates it for the remitter bank.
3. NPCI coordinates the debit leg against the payer's core banking system to lock and deduct the balance.
4. Once debit succeeds, NPCI executes the credit leg against the payee's bank, settling net balances later via central bank clearing.

If the credit leg fails midway, the switch initiates an automated reversal, preventing funds from vanishing between decoupled systems.
