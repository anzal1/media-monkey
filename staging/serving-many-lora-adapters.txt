Dynamic LoRA serving lets you host thousands of specialized fine-tuned models on a single GPU by sharing the base weights and hot-swapping lightweight delta matrices on the fly.

Here is where it bites:
→ Spinning up full 16-bit model weights for every single enterprise tenant
→ Crushing your memory bandwidth paging gigabytes of weights on cold starts
→ Watching GPU compute sit at five percent while VRAM stays maxed out

A full foundational model holds billions of fixed weights in memory. But a low-rank adapter only represents a tiny mathematical delta, often taking less than fifty megabytes of disk space.

Modern inference engines like S-LoRA and vLLM keep the giant base model pinned in VRAM, pool thousands of adapter weights in host system memory, and use custom CUDA kernels to batch inference requests across completely different adapters in a single pass.

You cut compute spend by over ninety percent while still offering custom fine-tuned behavior for every customer.
