Aisha taps send on a photo of her cat.
Before her brother's phone buzzes, WhatsApp media encryption splits the entire delivery in two.
The company's servers never actually see the image you shared 📸

WhatsApp handles media by completely separating the heavy file storage from the secret keys that unlock it.

Picture this:
→ You upload an eleven megabyte raw photo
→ The chat server sees pure random static
→ Only the recipient can assemble the pixels

1. Your phone compresses the photo locally and mints a unique 32-byte AES key in device memory.
2. The phone encrypts the image using AES-CBC with an HMAC, producing scrambled ciphertext and an integrity tag.
3. Your client uploads that encrypted blob directly to an HTTPS media server.
4. Your phone builds an end-to-end encrypted chat message containing the download URL, the integrity hash, and the decryption key.
5. That metadata travels across the Signal Protocol channel directly to your recipient.
6. The recipient phone downloads the scrambled blob and decrypts it directly in memory.

The media server hosts billions of encrypted blobs every single day, but lacks the cryptographic keys to render even a single preview thumbnail.

Next time you tap send, that photo is locked before it ever touches a radio tower.

Did you know your phone did all of that encryption locally before uploading?
