Priya taps send on a photo of her cat.
Before her brother's phone buzzes, WhatsApp media encryption splits the job into two completely different paths.
Meta never actually sees the image.

WhatsApp media encryption is a hybrid system where bulk files are encrypted with ephemeral keys and stored on standard media hosts, while the keys travel via the Signal Protocol.

Picture this:
→ You upload an uncompressed 10MB photo on shaky 4G
→ Passing that through an end-to-end messaging pipeline would stall the conversation
→ Storing unencrypted copies on cloud servers would violate privacy

1. Priya's phone compresses the photo and generates a random 32-byte AES key.
2. The phone encrypts the photo with AES-GCM and posts the ciphertext blob to a media storage server.
3. The storage server returns a content pointer and cryptographic hash.
4. Priya's device wraps that pointer, hash, and the AES key into a standard text envelope encrypted with the Signal Protocol.
5. Her brother's device receives the envelope, pulls the encrypted blob from the link, and unlocks it locally.

Meta handles millions of photo transfers a day without ever reading the raw image data on their central disks.

Next time you send a high-res photo across the country, check the speed.
